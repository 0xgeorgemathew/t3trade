How Hyperliquid Charges Fees

Hyperliquid is a USDC-margined perps DEX on its own L1. Trading is gasless; the only protocol-level charges are the maker/taker fee, funding, and a $1 withdrawal fee. Every number below is quoted from the official docs (citations at the bottom).

Three cost surfaces, not one.

1. Trading fee — maker or taker, charged on notional at both entry and exit. Tier-based, with staking and maker-volume modifiers.

2. Funding — peer-to-peer between longs and shorts, paid hourly, separate from fees. Sign depends on premium vs. oracle.

3. Liquidation — no clearance fee on HL (unlike CEXs). The only loss is the position's PnL down to maintenance margin.

Funding (separate from fees, paid hourly)

Computed as (8h rate) / 8, paid every hour. Positive funding → longs pay shorts. Capped at 4% / hour. The interest-rate floor in the formula is 0.01% / 8h. For a scalp held 3–60 min, funding is usually zero or one tick; it only bites if the bot holds across the hourly boundary in a skewed market.

Gasless trading.

There is no gas cost for placing, modifying, or cancelling orders on HyperCore (the L1 perps venue). Only withdrawals cost anything on-chain: a flat $1 fee to cover the Arbitrum bridge. Order churn is free.

How t3trade's Trades Map to Fee Events

The bot has no fee model of its own. It reads exchange fees and tracks them, but never marks a position for fees. So every fee below is realized in USDC on the exchange and silently reduces realized PnL — the bot's internal accounting doesn't see it.

The six trade actions and their fee character

Action What it does Order type Fee posture
open Start a new position (must carry a stop) IOC crossing or GTC resting Mostly taker; could earn maker only if GTC rests and fills
scale_in Add to an existing position IOC or GTC Same as open — taker-dominant
reduce Partial exit / bank profit Forced IOC 100% taker (hard-coded in TradingExecutionGuard)
close Flatten the whole position Forced IOC 100% taker
cancel Withdraw a resting order — No fee (cancels are free)
modify_stop Move the reduce-only stop cancel-and-replace No fee while resting; taker if it triggers
Every exit is a taker fill, by construction.

The execution guard forces orderPreference: "marketable_ioc" on every reduce and close regardless of what the strategy asked for. There is no post-only / ALO support anywhere in the wire layer. Model exits at the taker rate, always. Entries are taker-dominant too; the only path to a maker fill is a GTC resting order that doesn't cross — and the codebase doesn't track maker rebates, so the cost model prices everything at taker.

Protective stop = a hidden taker event

Every open and scale_in must attach a stop. The stop rests on-exchange as a reduce-only trigger with isMarket: true. While resting it costs nothing. If it triggers, it crosses as a taker fill on the full position notional — one extra fee event per losing trade. The stop fires at most once per position (it's reduce-only and flattens).

Holding period & funding exposure

The volatility horizons are 3–60 bars on a 1-minute timeframe, so the expected hold is 3–60 minutes. Funding is paid hourly, so most positions pay zero funding. The exception is a position open across the top of the hour in a skewed market — typically one hourly accrual, which on a $2,000 notional at the 0.01%/8h floor is ~$0.0025. Funding is not a meaningful cost surface for this strategy; trading fees are.

What This Means for the System

The bot's win-rate bar is silently higher than its accounting thinks.

Because the system has no fee model, every PnL figure the harness computes is before exchange fees. On the realistic $800 mandate, a round-trip win must clear $0.72 in fees just to break even against the bot's own books — that's 0.72% of margin per round trip of invisible drag. On the $2,000 reference it's $1.80 / 1.80%.

Three concrete takeaways

  • Exits are 100% taker and cannot be optimized away. The execution guard forces IOC on every reduce and close. Fee reduction is only possible on the entry leg, and only if the strategy switches to resting GTC orders that actually fill without crossing.
  • Stop triggers are an unbudgeted fee event. Every losing trade that hits its stop pays a second taker fee on the full notional. The bot doesn't model this; a backtest without it will understate losing-trade cost by ~$0.90 on $2,000 notional (or $0.36 on the real $800).
  • Fees scale with churn, not position size. A scale-in / partial-out / close flow (Scenario C) doubles the fee count. The most fee-efficient shape is a single entry → single exit; the ladder / scale-out path costs proportionally more.

What is NOT a cost surface here

Don't over-model these — they're negligible for this strategy.

Gas: zero on HyperCore. Order churn, cancels, stop modifications are all free.

Funding: near-zero at 3–60 min holds. Only matters if the bot starts holding across hourly boundaries in skewed markets.

Liquidation clearance: $0 on HL. If a position liquidates, the dominant loss is the position PnL down to maintenance margin (~1.25% of notional on ETH), not a fee.
